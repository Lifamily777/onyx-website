# ONYX Founder Specification — v1

Status: **Foundational specification. Not yet implemented.**
Scope: Strategic, brand, content, design, and technical foundation for the ONYX website (www.onyxww.com) and connected channels.

This document is the long-term source of truth for ONYX. Any future redesign, content work, or feature build on this project should be checked against this spec before implementation. Changes to this document should be deliberate — treat it as versioned (`v1`, `v2`, …) rather than silently edited.

## 2. The Deeper Purpose

ONYX was inspired by a personal realization: professional achievement should not require choosing between a meaningful career and being present for the people we love.

The founder is a daughter, wife, mother, professional, entrepreneur, and lifelong learner. She left her parents and home in her twenties to pursue her career. During her thirties, she worked full-time while raising children and missed meaningful moments in their growth. She is deeply grateful for the support of her family and recognizes that her professional journey would not have been possible without them.

ONYX exists partly because families should not have to spend their limited lives lost in unnecessary complexity, information overload, administrative confusion, and constant busyness. The platform aims to help families spend less time overwhelmed by systems and more time with the people, purposes, and experiences that matter most.

Knowledge reduces uncertainty. Wisdom reduces entropy.

Without intentional understanding and maintenance:

- financial decisions become fragmented,
- health habits deteriorate,
- information becomes outdated,
- technology becomes overwhelming,
- family time disappears,
- and daily life gradually becomes more disorganized.

**However, entropy should not be used as the primary public-facing marketing language unless it is clearly explained.**

The public promise is:

> We reduce entropy. We transform complexity into clarity. We preserve human judgment in the age of AI.

## 4. Audience

ONYX primarily serves hardworking families and lifelong learners from different backgrounds, potentially including:

- middle-class families,
- working parents,
- immigrants,
- first-generation families,
- professionals,
- small-business owners,
- career changers,
- adults supporting children or aging parents,
- people trying to understand unfamiliar financial, health, insurance, tax, or technology systems,
- and people who feel they work hard but do not fully understand why the results remain stressful or uncertain.

Their problem is not laziness. Many already work extremely hard. Their problem is that modern systems are fragmented, technical, rapidly changing, and rarely explained as a connected whole.

**Common audience thoughts include:**

- "I work hard, but I still do not feel secure."
- "I pay taxes, but I do not understand the rules."
- "I have insurance, but I do not know exactly what it protects."
- "I save for retirement, but I do not know whether everything works together."
- "I care about my health, but I am overwhelmed by conflicting information."
- "I want to use AI, but I do not want to lose my judgment."
- "I cannot become an expert in everything, but I still have to make important decisions."

The audience should feel respected, not patronized. The website should communicate:

> "You belong here. We are learning together."

## 6. Core Pillars

ONYX has three connected pillars.

### Wealth

**Purpose:** Understanding the financial systems shaping family life.

Topics may include: tax education, EA study journey, insurance literacy, retirement systems, estate planning, family financial organization, business structures, risk management, investing concepts, ownership, and long-term financial decision-making.

**Positioning:** Not "get rich quickly." Not "beat the IRS." Not "secret tax loopholes." The focus is understanding rules, incentives, tradeoffs, risks, and choices.

### Wellness

**Purpose:** Understanding the biological and behavioral systems that support family health.

Topics may include: nutrition education, preventive health, healthy routines, exercise, swimming, sleep, sustainable habits, longevity, family wellness, food choices, product research, and the founder's personal wellness-learning journey.

**Positioning:** Not perfection. Not miracle claims. Not medical diagnosis. Not product-first selling. The focus is learning, evidence, habits, prevention, and practical decision-making.

### Intelligence

**Purpose:** Understanding technological, cognitive, and decision systems.

Topics may include: AI literacy, Claude, ChatGPT, Cursor, AI tools, automation, digital strategy, finance transformation, decision intelligence, systems thinking, personal knowledge management, learning methods, and responsible use of technology.

**Positioning:** Not AI hype. Not replacing human judgment. Not claiming that one tool solves everything. The focus is using technology responsibly to learn, work, decide, and adapt more effectively.

## 8. Commercial Philosophy

ONYX is intended to become a sustainable business. Potential income streams may eventually include: insurance services (where properly licensed), USANA product sales, books, courses, digital products, consulting, speaking, affiliate partnerships, advertising or sponsorships, community or membership offerings, and other relevant educational or commercial services.

- health miracle claims,
- tax avoidance rhetoric,
- fear-based selling,
- pressure tactics,
- fake urgency,
- hidden conflicts of interest,
- or claims beyond the founder's qualifications.

The platform should build trust while also supporting legitimate business growth.

## 9. Content Model

ONYX is a learning-in-public platform. The founder studies a topic, reflects on it, tests or applies it when appropriate, then shares what appears useful.

**Use verbs such as:** study, explore, document, translate, connect, compare, test, reflect, organize, share.

**Avoid repeatedly claiming:** "I teach everything," "I know the answer," "This is the only correct method," or "Follow me because I am the expert."

The content system should support the first 100 genuine learning cycles. Each learning cycle should include:

1. A real question.
2. Study notes.
3. What was learned.
4. Why it matters.
5. Common misconceptions.
6. Risks, limitations, or uncertainty.
7. Practical next steps.
8. Sources or evidence where appropriate.
9. A website article.
10. One or more channel-specific adaptations.

## 11. Multilingual Strategy

- **English** is the default and primary language.
- Support: English, Simplified Chinese, Spanish, Korean.
- Language labels: English · 中文 · Español · 한국어
- Add a clearly visible but elegant language selector in the header and mobile navigation.
- Implement proper internationalization rather than hard-coding four duplicate websites.

**Preferred URL structure:** `/`, `/zh`, `/es`, `/ko`

Use the existing project framework's recommended internationalization method.

**Requirements:**

- All homepage text must be stored in translation files or structured locale resources.
- Language selection should preserve the current equivalent page when possible.
- The selected language should persist during navigation.
- The design must support longer translations without breaking layouts.
- The HTML language attribute must update correctly.
- Metadata should be localized.
- Add appropriate hreflang implementation where technically suitable.
- English should be used as the fallback language.
- The system must allow human-reviewed translations later.
- Do not depend exclusively on runtime machine translation.
- Future articles should be able to indicate whether a translation is human-reviewed, AI-assisted, pending, or unavailable.
- Do not fabricate translated articles where no approved translation exists.

**Multilingual principle:** Knowledge should never be limited by language. Translations involving tax, insurance, health, legal, or financial terminology must prioritize meaning, context, and cultural clarity over literal word-for-word translation.

## 14. Tone of Voice

The ONYX voice should be: intelligent, grounded, warm, direct, curious, strategic, calm, credible, practical, humble, accessible. May be thoughtful and occasionally witty, but not flippant about serious matters.

Use plain language without oversimplifying important concepts. Respect the reader's intelligence. Use "we," "us," and "together" where appropriate. Do not sound as if the founder is speaking down to the audience.

**Avoid:** hype, exaggerated promises, fake certainty, aggressive sales language, corporate jargon, generic coaching language, fear-based messaging, "financial freedom" clichés, "secret loopholes," "beat the system," "hack the IRS," miracle-wellness claims, AI-utopia claims, empty inspiration.

## 15. Visual Direction

**The design should feel:** modern, editorial, premium, calm, human, intelligent, timeless, accessible, internationally relevant.

**Avoid:** generic financial-adviser imagery, luxury-wealth clichés, excessive gold, generic smiling-family stock photos, generic AI robots, glowing brains, heavy gradients, excessive glassmorphism, excessive cards, startup-dashboard aesthetics, MLM-style layouts, aggressive sales funnels, overly feminine or masculine stereotypes.

**Preferred visual qualities:** generous whitespace, strong typography, restrained details, subtle motion, clear information hierarchy, elegant contrast, layouts that work across languages.

**Potential palette direction** if the current palette requires refinement: charcoal, deep black, warm white, stone, muted gold (used sparingly), dark green, restrained neutral accents.

**Do not replace an existing coherent design system without a clear reason.**

---

## 16. Typography

Use a refined display typeface for major editorial headlines if it fits the current project and does not introduce unnecessary dependency or performance cost. Use a highly readable sans-serif typeface for navigation, body copy, buttons, metadata, and translated content.

**Ensure:** clear hierarchy, accessible font sizes, sufficient line height, readable mobile typography, support for Latin characters, Simplified Chinese, Korean, and accented Spanish characters.

Do not select fonts that lack reliable multilingual glyph coverage.

---

## 17. Accessibility

Target strong practical accessibility (WCAG 2.1 AA where reasonably achievable).

**Requirements:** semantic HTML, logical heading hierarchy, keyboard-operable navigation, accessible language selector, visible focus states, sufficient color contrast, meaningful button and link labels, appropriate alt text, reduced-motion support, no essential information conveyed only by color, correctly updated document language, screen-reader-friendly navigation.

## 21. Technical Principles

Work within the existing project. Do not rebuild from scratch. Do not change the framework unless explicitly authorized.

**Do not change** (unless necessary and clearly documented):

- deployment configuration,
- domain configuration,
- repository structure,
- routing strategy,
- environment variables,
- external service integrations,
- or existing working functionality.

**Before implementation:** inspect the project structure, identify the framework, identify the homepage, identify shared layouts, identify the current styling system, identify existing components, identify routing, identify current dependencies, identify current localization support, identify available fonts and icons, and identify build, lint, and type-check commands.

**Prefer:** reuse, clean components, configuration-driven content, maintainable locale files, responsive design, semantic markup, minimal new dependencies.

Do not add a dependency when the existing stack already provides the required capability.

## 23. Implementation Workflow

After creating this specification file, do not immediately redesign the website. First:

1. Inspect the current repository.
2. Produce a concise current-state assessment.
3. Identify the exact files likely to be modified.
4. Explain the current framework and styling approach.
5. Identify risks or conflicts with this specification.
6. Recommend the implementation sequence.
7. Identify any decisions that genuinely require founder input.
8. Do not ask questions whose answers can be reasonably inferred from this specification or the existing code.

Then wait for implementation authorization.
